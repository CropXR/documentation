# Frequently Asked Questions

??? question "What are the levels of data sharing?"

    Data and metadata can be shared at three levels: your work package, the CropXR consortium (through the reporting procedure), and the public or third parties (through the publication procedure). What is shared by default, and who decides, is different for metadata and for data.

    **Metadata (the description of your data in the data catalogue)**

    - By default, your metadata is shared with your work package.
    - You can change who sees it yourself. You can share it with specific people or groups of people. To share it with other work packages, share it with their project in the data catalogue. To share it with the whole consortium, share it with the CropXR programme.

    **Data (the files you upload to the Resilience Hub)**

    - By default, your data is not shared with anyone other than you and the Gatekeeper.
    - Only the Gatekeeper, who is the work package lead or a PI, can share your data with others. To share data outside of your work package, the reporting or publication procedure applies.

??? question "Who gets access to my data, and to my metadata, if I upload it to the Resilience Hub?"

    **Your data**

    At first, only you and the Gatekeeper (the work package lead or a PI) can access it. The Gatekeeper decides who else gets access. Others log in, accept the Resilience Hub's Terms and Conditions and submit an access request, which the Gatekeeper evaluates. Requests are handled within 3 months at the latest.

    **Your metadata**

    The metadata you register in the data catalogue is shared with your work package by default. You can change this yourself. We encourage you to share the metadata of your experiments, because it fosters data collaboration.

??? question "What should I upload to the Resilience Hub?"

    Follow the community-driven standard. Upload your raw data, so that your results can be reproduced, and processed data that is useful to others, especially data that is hard to produce again. There are no strict rules yet, and the consortium is developing standards. See [What Data to Upload](concepts/what-data-to-upload.md) for examples.

    Upload as soon as possible: the Resilience Hub is more secure than a laptop and backs up your data automatically.

??? question "How do I get access to the Resilience Hub?"

    If you are a member of CropXR, log in with your institution's credentials through Keycloak. If you have problems, contact the DataXR team at [data@cropxr.org](mailto:data@cropxr.org).

    Researchers outside CropXR can request access by emailing the Data Access Committee with the purpose of their request. They can currently get access to the file services only.

??? question "Do I have to re-enter metadata in the data catalogue that I already have in another version of the metadata template in the Research Drive?"

    Yes, the metadata needs to be entered in the data catalogue. However, the DataXR team can help you transfer your existing metadata, so that duplicated effort is kept to a minimum. Contact the DataXR team at [data@cropxr.org](mailto:data@cropxr.org).

??? question "Should I wait for a better tool or a new template before documenting my metadata?"

    No. It is always better to start documenting your metadata as soon as possible. We are developing tools to help you document your data, but we do not know yet when they will be released. Whatever you document in the meantime helps us develop and test those tools faster.

??? question "What are an investigation, a study and an assay?"

    The consortium's conceptual model is inspired by the ISA (Investigation, Study, Assay) framework, with elements from MIAPPE for plant phenotyping and ENA for sequencing data. In short:

    - An investigation is a broader research theme, typically covering the work of a PI or several researchers. It contains one or more studies.
    - A study is a collection of related experiments that together address a research question, roughly equivalent in scope to a publication.
    - An assay is a measurement or analytical process performed on samples.

    The full definitions, and other terms, are in the [glossary](getting-started/glossary.md).

    If you find it hard to map your research onto these terms, contact the DataXR team at [data@cropxr.org](mailto:data@cropxr.org) and they will help you.

??? question "Who can help me with metadata and data questions (data manager or data steward)?"

    Your main point of contact for questions about data or metadata is [data@cropxr.org](mailto:data@cropxr.org). Someone from the DataXR team will follow up on your question, depending on who is best placed to help with your case.

    You can also contact one of our data stewards directly: Eva Ferradosa (PhD researcher on metadata documentation and part-time data steward), [e.e.ferradosa@tudelft.nl](mailto:e.e.ferradosa@tudelft.nl). A second, full-time data steward will join soon.

??? question "Do I have to fill in every field of the metadata template?"

    No. Mandatory metadata fields are signaled as you document them in the data catalogue. How far you go beyond that will depend on what you want people to be able to do with your data. Findability needs only a title, description, and keywords. Reusability by people or pipelines needs more granular, standardised information. Data becomes more valuable when the purpose and design of the experiment are described.
